Temperature forecasting

The path ahead.

We develop models for how temperature may evolve over the rest of a local day, including the eventual daily maximum. A useful forecast describes the plausible range and how that range changes as new evidence arrives.

  • Remaining intraday temperature paths
  • Daily maximum temperature distributions
  • Uncertainty at different forecast horizons
Weather-market analysis

The outcome in context.

We develop software to interpret forecasts against weather prediction-market outcomes. Each question has a location, time window and settlement definition; these details determine what a forecast needs to predict.

  • City and local-day alignment
  • Temperature thresholds and outcome ranges
  • Settlement-aware analysis and trading research

Our approach

Evidence first. Then a forecast.

01 /

Preserve the evidence

Keep weather observations and forecast inputs tied to their sources, revisions and availability times, so experiments can distinguish what was known when.

02 /

Model possible outcomes

Develop probabilistic forecasts for temperature paths and daily maxima. AWS supports data preparation, machine learning experiments and forecast evaluation.

03 /

Study decisions in context

Build software for weather prediction-market analysis and trading research, connecting forecast distributions with specific city, date and settlement definitions.

Research standards

Designed to be questioned.
Built to be evaluated.

01

Respect the clock

Experiments should use information available at the decision time. Publication, observation and ingestion times matter when evaluating a forecast fairly.

02

Match the target

A station reading, a local-day maximum and a market settlement are related but distinct. We make the forecast target explicit before interpreting an outcome.

03

Measure uncertainty

We study forecast distributions, comparison baselines and observed outcomes. Evaluation should show where a model helps and where uncertainty remains.

04

Keep work reproducible

Source identity, input versions and experiment records connect a result to its evidence. This makes findings easier to inspect, compare and revisit.

Questions driving the work

What changes when
new evidence arrives?

Our research explores how forecasts respond to the unfolding day and how to evaluate that response.

  1. How much additional warming remains plausible?

    Study the remaining temperature path in the context of observations and forecast inputs available so far.

  2. How should probabilities change as the day progresses?

    Examine updates to daily maximum distributions as material weather evidence arrives.

  3. Does the forecast hold up on later, unseen days?

    Use chronological evaluation and relevant comparison baselines to test findings beyond the data used for development.
