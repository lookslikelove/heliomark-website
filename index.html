The forecasting question

How hot could it get?
How likely is it?

For a weather-market decision, the likely daily high is only part of the picture. The range of possible highs, the chance of each outcome, and the evidence that could change that view all matter.

We develop forecasts that describe those possibilities. Our work brings together observations, forecast guidance and machine learning to estimate how temperatures may change through the day and what that means for the daily high.

Research approach

How we test
a forecast.

We evaluate forecasts on later days the model has not seen. The aim is to understand when probabilities are useful, where they fall short, and how new evidence should change them.

  • 01Match the forecast to the market’s temperature target.
  • 02Use weather data available at forecast time.
  • 03Compare with observed highs and established forecasts.
  • 04Study errors across different weather conditions.

As the day unfolds

New observations.
New expectations.

Cloud cover, a clearing sky or a stronger breeze can change the temperature outlook. We study when new observations should shift a forecast and what that could mean for a weather-market decision.

  • 01How much time and sunlight remain for further warming?
  • 02Has the likely high moved into a different market range?
  • 03Does the new evidence justify changing the forecast?
